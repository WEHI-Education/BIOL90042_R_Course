23  Tutorial 9: BIOL90042 final project

In this tutorial, we will introduce the final group project for BIOL90042, where you will analyse several related datasets in the context of a research program on Parkinson’s Disease.

23.1 Background

23.1.1 Parkinson’s Disease

Parkinson’s Disease (PD) is a neurodegenerative condition caused by the progressive loss of dopamine-producing neurons in a region of the brain called the substantia nigra. Before these neurons die, misfolded alpha-synuclein protein starts to build up inside them and this build-up, along with mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress and neuroinflammation, seems to play a key role in driving the disease.

Patients with PD experience motor symptoms such as slowed movement, tremor, poor balance, an unsteady gait and more frequent falls. Non-motor symptoms like depression, fatigue, cognitive impairment, psychotic symptoms and dementia are also common, and can be just as disruptive to a patient’s quality of life.

What causes PD is not fully understood. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified many common genetic variants (SNPs) that are more common in PD patients than in the general population. Patients having many of these common SNPs are considered to have a high polygenic risk. A smaller group of patients (around 10%) instead carry one of a handful of rare, high-risk SNPs that substantially raise their chances of developing PD. For everyone else, the disease is considered ‘sporadic’, meaning it is not clearly tied to genetics, but might instead be linked to long-term exposure to pesticides, heavy metals or air pollution.

PD motor symptoms can be managed in the early disease stage using the dopamine supplement L-DOPA, however treatments to prevent neuronal cell death are needed, and to date there is no cure for PD.

23.1.2 Project scenario

In this project, your group is a team of biomedical researchers tasked with analysing the data from WEHI’s Parkinson’s Disease research program, which has the following four arms: 1. Population genetics 2. RNA-seq of patient iPSC-derived neurons 3. Drug screening 4. Clinical trial

As a group, you will need to use R to analyse data from these four arms, and present them in both a quarto report (.qmd, combining code, plots and explanations) and oral presentation. Participants are identified by a unique donor id (donorid column in the datasets), and some participants took part in multiple study arms, so you may use them to connect the different datasets.

23.2.2 RNA-seq of patient iPSC-derived neurons

This data is in the rnsaeq.Rda (DGEList) and rnaseq_gene_info.Rda (gene names and gene ontology data).

23.2.4 Clinical trial

This data is in the gait.Rda file.

It turns out that one of the hits from the drug screen is already approved (for other diseases) by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration. Thus, a clinical trial is designed to test the effect of this drug on PD motor symptoms. 16 PD patients are andomly assigned to L-DOPA (current standard of care), or the trial drug. In addition, half of the patients in each treatment group are randomly assigned to a supervised daily exercise routine.

All patients are given a smart watch to wear that measures their vertical orientation (in degrees off-axis) whilst walking throughout the day (every second, for 700 seconds). This measurement (in the 1_secs, 2_secs, etc columns), is a proxy for balance and smoothness of motion whilst walking. A measurement of 0 in indicates that the patient is standing perfectly upright.

Note that watches are not worn during the exercise routine, just during everyday activities.

23.3 Research questions

You are welcome to explore the data as you wish, but the following questions should be answered for each dataset in your quarto report. Due to time constraints for the oral presentation, you will need to choose what you think is the most interesting/relevant to present

23.3.1 Population genetics

  1. Calculate a PRS for each participant. Which participant has the highest PRS? The lowest?
  2. Plot and describe (with descriptive statistics) the distribution of PRS for both cases and controls.
  3. Is there a statistically significant difference in the PRS of cases and controls? Ensure you check all the assumptions of the test you choose.

23.3.2 RNA-seq of patient iPSC-derived neurons

  1. Which genes are differentially expressed between PD cases and healthy controls? Please also visualise your DE results with appropriate plot(s).
  2. Which gene ontology families do the DE genes belong to?
  3. Is the clustering of samples by gene expression consistent with their disease status as cases and controls?

23.3.3 Drug screening

  1. Using the geom_tile() function, visualise the luminescence across the plate. Do there appear to be any spatial patterns in this data?
  2. Which compounds are statistically significantly associated with increased neuronal viability after MPP exposure? Ensure that you check your assumptions.
  3. What are the mechanisms of action and targets of the compounds you identify in Q2?

23.3.4 Clinical trial

  1. Plot each participants’ gait data and describe it using descriptive statistics. Which of these descriptive statistics do you think is most useful for capturing the overall gait smoothness and balance of a participant?
  2. Is there a statistically significant difference in gait between participants receiving L-DOPA and the new compound? Ensure that you check your assumptions.
  3. Does either of age or the exercise regime affect gait? If so, take this into consideration when investigating the relationship between the treatment and gait.

23.3.5 Bringing it together/linking between datasets

  1. Using their unique donor IDs, how many patients appear across all four arms of the study?
  2. Do any variables other than gene expression distinguish the RNA-seq cases from controls? (Hint: try colouring points on your MDS plot by other information you have about the donors, from the other datasets)
  3. Are any of the gene targets of hit compounds from the drug screen, differentially expressed in RNA-seq? Which GO terms are they associated with and what might this tell you about the pathology of PD?
  4. Is there any relationship between the PRS and gait of patients in the clinical trial?
  5. In a paragraph, summarise your findings and suggest directions for future research.

23.4 Deliverables

Each group will need to produce the following:

23.4.1 Quarto report (code)

You should begin by making an R project for your group (see Section 6.1 for instructions on how to do this) where you will store all of your data, code and any plots/outputs you save. Then, make a quarto report (.qmd file) that contains both your analysis code and explanations/interpretations of your findings. You need to submit the entire R project, as a zip file so that we can test your code on the data.

Tips for code/organisation:

  • You may produce multiple reports (for example one for each dataset) if this is easier for you to manage within your groups, as long as they are clearly named

  • If you find it helpful, you are welcome to work within git (see Chapter 18) but this is not mandatory

  • Ensure all of your code is well-commented and the logic behind what you’re doing is clear

  • It is expected that all of the research questions will be addressed in your report.

Tips for data visualisation:

  • Make sure the colour schemes are consistent (e.g. PD cases and healthy controls should always use the same colours across all plots)

  • Make sure you label your plots nicely (headings, axis labels)

  • Particularly for the oral presentation, make sure that the font size on the plots is big enough that we can easily read it on the slide

23.4.2 Oral presentation

In the final tutorial (week 12), we will go over some tips for science communication. In general though:

  • Due to the time limit, it won’t be possible to include all of your results. Just choose a selection of the things you found most interesting and focus on presenting those clearly without rushing

  • Slides should focus primarily on graphical representations of the data, with summary statistics or statistical test results (e.g. p value) included as appropriate

  • Be prepared to answer questions about your approach to answering the questions (code), as well as your interpretation of the results

23.5 A note on AI usage

You are permitted to use AI to help you with this assessment, but remember that you are responsible for your code, so please check anything you get from AI thoroughly! Also, if your code isn’t commented clearly, or you can’t answer questions about why you chose a certain approach (particularly over one we covered in class), you will lose marks regardless of whether or not the output is correct, so you need to understand the code you submit.

### Parkinson's Disease

progressive loss of dopamine-producing neurons in a region of the brain
called the substantia nigra. Before these neurons die, misfolded
alpha-synuclein protein starts to build up inside them and this
build-up, along with mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress and
neuroinflammation, seems to play a key role in driving the disease.

Patients with PD experience motor symptoms such as slowed movement,
tremor, poor balance, an unsteady gait and more frequent falls.
Non-motor symptoms like depression, fatigue, cognitive impairment,
psychotic symptoms and dementia are also common, and can be just as
disruptive to a patient's quality of life.

What causes PD is not fully understood. Genome-wide association studies
(GWAS) have identified many common genetic variants (SNPs) that are more
common in PD patients than in the general population. Patients having
many of these common SNPs are considered to have a high polygenic risk.

of rare, high-risk SNPs that substantially raise their chances of
developing PD. For everyone else, the disease is considered ‘sporadic’,

to long-term exposure to pesticides, heavy metals or air pollution.

PD motor symptoms can be managed in the early disease stage using the
dopamine supplement L-DOPA, however treatments to prevent neuronal cell
death are needed, and to date there is no cure for PD.

In this project, your group is a team of biomedical researchers tasked

### Population genetics

This data is in the `prs.Rda` file.

A GWAS has been performed on Australian PD patients (cases) and healthy
controls. The top hits are summarised in the `gwas_top_hits` dataframe:

```{r}
gwas_top_hits
```

For each of these 15 loci, the reference and alternative alleles are
given, and a GWAS risk coefficient (`beta` column) has been calculated.
You will need this information to calculate polygenic risk scores (PRS)
for the patients in this study.

The genotypes of 150 PD patients and 150 healthy controls at these 15
loci were determined, and are provided in the `ca_co_genotypes`
dataframe:

```{r}
ca_co_genotypes
```

There is one column for each risk locus, and the values in these columns
represent the 'risk genotype dosage' (how many copies of the risky
allele an individual has: 0, 1 or 2).

To calculate the PRS of an individual, take the sum of each locus' risk
genotype dosage by the corresponding GWAS risk coefficient.

More information about GWAS can be found in lecture 7.

### RNA-seq of patient iPSC-derived neurons

This data is in the `rnsaeq.Rda` (DGEList) and `rnaseq_gene_info.Rda`
(gene names and gene ontology data).

To compare the transcriptional profiles of PD neurons compared to
healthy controls, induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from 8 PD
patients and 8 healthy age and sex-matched controls were differentiated
into dopaminergic neurons. After two weeks in culture, RNA was isolated,
sequenced and pre-processed to generate the DGEList object, `dge`.

You are also provided with `entrez_genename_key`, a dataframe that
connects the [Entrez
IDs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1761442/) of genes (as
given in `dge$genes`) to the actual gene names and description:

```{r}
entrez_genename_key
```

[Gene ontology (GO)](https://geneontology.org/) information, which
describes the broad biological functions of genes is provided in the
`gene_ontology` dataframe:

```{r}
gene_ontology
```

GO is useful for interpreting the your differential expression (DE)
results, without needing to do extensive research on all of your DE
genes.

More information about RNA-seq analysis can be found in workshop 7 (see
@sec-workshop07).

### Drug screening

This data is in the `drugscreen.Rda` file.

To identify chemical compounds that can protect neurons from cytotoxic
stress, a drug screen was performed using a 1536-well plate containing
dopaminergic neurons from a healthy donor. 192 test compounds, and 192
control compounds were each tested in technical quadruplicate (i.e.,
each compound in 4 separate wells). The compound in each cell is
described in the `plate_meta` dataframe:

```{r}
plate_meta
```

Each test compound has a control which has the same molecular weight and
molecular constituents, but different chemistry with no known biological
activity. The enzyme target and mechanism of action for each of the test
compounds is known, and described in the `compound_dict` dataframe:

```{r}
compound_dict
```

After adding the compound to the neurons, all wells were treated with
MPP, a chemical that causes oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage to
mimick PD disease biology. After 24 hours of incubation, neuronal
viability was measured in a luminescence assay. Cells that survived and
grew despite MPP exposure have the highest luminescence values, whereas
those damaged by MPP have lower luminescence values. These luminescence
values are given in relative light units (RLU) in the `plate1`
dataframe:

```{r}
plate1
```

### Clinical trial

This data is in the `gait.Rda` file.

It turns out that one of the hits from the drug screen is already
approved (for other diseases) by the Australian Therapeutic Goods
Administration. Thus, a clinical trial is designed to test the effect of
this drug on PD motor symptoms. 16 PD patients are andomly assigned to
L-DOPA (current standard of care), or the trial drug. In addition, half
of the patients in each treatment group are randomly assigned to a
supervised daily exercise routine.

All patients are given a smart watch to wear that measures their
vertical orientation (in degrees off-axis) whilst walking throughout the
day (every second, for 700 seconds). This measurement (in the `1_secs`,
`2_secs`, etc columns), is a proxy for balance and smoothness of motion
whilst walking. A measurement of 0 in indicates that the patient is
standing perfectly upright.

Note that watches are not worn during the exercise routine, just during
everyday activities.

```{r}
gait_data
```
